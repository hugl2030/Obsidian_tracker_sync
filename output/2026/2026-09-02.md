Abstract 
                   Silicon-based anodes offer substantial potential for increasing batteries’ energy density but are limited by unstable solid-electrolyte interphases (SEIs). Here, we report a chemo-electrochemical tandem reduction strategy to construct an integrated eupolymer-inorganic SEI with both favorable mechanical robustness and fast Li+ transport. This strategy couples a selective ester–amine exchange reaction in the bulk electrolyte with a subsequent electrochemical reduction at the anode surface. This work provides profound insights into SEI tailoring, offering pathway toward long-life, fast-charging silicon-based batteries.

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Lithium-rich oxides (LROs) deliver an energy density of over 1000 Wh kg−1 for lithium-ion battery cathode applications, yet cycling-induced stress accumulation triggers structural degradation and poor cyclic stability. Herein, hierarchical twin structures are prefabricated in LROs. Prestress at twin boundaries forms interlaced stress networks to adaptively accommodate tensile/compressive strains upon cycling and dissipate localized stress concentration. The self-adaptive twin structure tolerates cyclic volume fluctuation, preserving the layered framework against irreversible spinel/rock-salt phase transition during long-term structural relaxation. The twin-modified LRO retains 94.98% capacity after 500 cycles (1 C) and 80.79% after 800 cycles, with 155.31 mAh g−1 at 5 C. The pouch cell delivers 12.59 Ah discharge capacity and maintains 84.83% capacity after 49 cycles. This work offers a strategy to tune electrochemo-mechanical properties for designing cathode materials.

Abstract 
                   Interfacial water structure is theoretically envisioned as the master regulator of electrocatalytic reaction kinetics, but the direct experimental validation of this role has remained a formidable challenge.
