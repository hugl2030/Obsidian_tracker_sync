### 1. Understanding the Role of Lithium Metal Microstructure in Chemo-Mechanical Instability of Solid-State Batteries

> **了解锂金属微结构在固态电池化学机械不稳定性中的作用**

| **日期** | 2026-10-02 |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Increased energy density and safety relative to conventional lithium (Li)-ion batteries motivate the development of solid-state batteries (SSBs). However, maintaining interface stability at the metal anode–solid electrolyte (SE) interface remains a critical challenge to achieving practical areal capacities, especially during stripping/discharge. Here, a mesoscale model informed by atomistic simulations is employed to examine void formation at the Li metal–SE interface during discharge/stripping. The model reveals how void-formation-driven interfacial contact loss behavior depends on the microstructural properties of the polycrystalline Li anode. In agreement with prior studies, Li microstructures with small grain sizes promote self-diffusion-induced void healing and generally exhibit better interfacial contact. We further show the effect of operating conditions, such as applied pressure, stripping current density, and temperature, on the stripping performance for different Li microstructures. Through this study, we shed light on the importance of taking microstructural features into account in predicting interfacial contact evolution during stripping of Li metal SSBs.

### 2. Modeling Approaches for Polymer-Based Electrolytes in Advanced Batteries: Bridging Processing, Structure, and Performance

> **高级电池中聚合物基电解质的建模方法：桥接处理、结构和性能**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Ying Zhang, Pei Sun, Giuseppe A. Elia, Alejandro A. Franco et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-10-02 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02246](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02246) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Polymer-based electrolytes are increasingly important in advanced batteries because of their structural tunability, mechanical compliance, and compatibility with diverse electrode and cell architectures. Their behavior depends on transport, interfacial, mechanical, and structural processes that span multiple scales and are shaped by formulation and processing history. This Perspective examines how modeling can be applied to polymer-based electrolyte systems in batteries and introduces a framework based on modeling objective, modeling basis, characteristic scale or data level, and degree of process coupling. Battery-relevant ionogel studies are used to map current modeling emphases and underrepresented directions, while polymer-electrolyte-containing composite electrodes illustrate challenges associated with process-generated microstructure and multifunctional polymer phases. Broader gaps are identified in material-state definition, experiment-model information transfer, structural representation, and process coupling. Progress therefore depends not simply on adding isolated models but on developing transparent, experimentally anchored workflows that establish testable links among formulation, processing, structure, transport, and battery-relevant performance.

Abstract 
                   Redox-active ligands are well established for enabling open-shell reactivity in first-row transition-metal catalysis. Despite the privileged role of 2,2′-bipyridine (bpy) ligands in homogeneous catalysis, bpy-supported half-sandwich organoiron complexes have remained essentially unexplored. ### 2. Multi-Field
CEST NMR Reconstructs a Protein Folding
Landscape

> **多字段
CEST NMR重建蛋白质折叠
横向**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Ved Prakash Tiwari, Laetitia Rožić, Sebastian Hiller |
| **日期** | 2026-10-02 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c16569](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c16569) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Protein dynamics are central to their folding and function, yet the sparsely populated conformational states that underpin these processes remain difficult to characterize at atomic resolution. Chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) NMR can detect such states and is particularly sensitive to rapid exchange between two minor conformations. This “minor-state exchange”, however, broadens and displaces the minor-state dip in CEST profiles, leading to parameter degeneracy that can preclude quantitative reconstruction of multistate exchange networks. Here, we show that, in the presence of rapid “minor-exchange”, the apparent minor-state chemical shift obtained from two-state fits varies systematically with the static magnetic field, B0. We exploit this field dependence to develop a nonperturbative, multi-field CEST strategy that distinguishes otherwise degenerate exchange models. Applying multi-field 15N-CEST to the model protein Im7 revealed a folding intermediate populated at ∼1% that exchanges with the folded state on a ∼2 ms time scale and with an unfolded state populated at ∼0.4% on a ∼200 μs time scale. Although the unfolded state does not produce a separate CEST dip, its exchange is encoded in the line width and B0-dependent position of the intermediate-state dip. Analysis of the 15N-CEST data provides populations, microscopic exchange rates, and residue-specific 15N chemical shifts for all three states. The resulting Im7 free-energy landscape is sequential, with folding proceeding through the intermediate and no detectable direct exchange between the folded and unfolded states. Multi-field CEST provides a broadly applicable, perturbation-free approach for resolving biomolecular dynamics at equilibrium.

                   Complex fluids transition from laminar to transitional flow above a critical control parameter, in a similar manner to their Newtonian counterparts. In a continuum mechanics sense, fluid elements follow the ensuing complex trajectories, which gives rise to secondary flows in terms of macroscopic vortices. However, it is not known whether their trajectories exhibit the same spatiotemporal behaviour as the macroscopic flow field if idealized fluid elements are replaced with actual anisotropic nanoparticles. Here, to address this fundamental question, we develop a multiscale method that combines small-angle X-ray scatter microscopy with polarized light imaging, thereby bridging seven orders of magnitude in length scales. We demonstrate proof of principle for Taylor–Couette flow—a classical transitional flow problem—of platelet-like graphene oxide nanoparticle and rod-like cellulose nanocrystal suspensions.
