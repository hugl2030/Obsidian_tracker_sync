**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Increased energy density and safety relative to conventional lithium (Li)-ion batteries motivate the development of solid-state batteries (SSBs). However, maintaining interface stability at the metal anode–solid electrolyte (SE) interface remains a critical challenge to achieving practical areal capacities, especially during stripping/discharge. Here, a mesoscale model informed by atomistic simulations is employed to examine void formation at the Li metal–SE interface during discharge/stripping. The model reveals how void-formation-driven interfacial contact loss behavior depends on the microstructural properties of the polycrystalline Li anode. In agreement with prior studies, Li microstructures with small grain sizes promote self-diffusion-induced void healing and generally exhibit better interfacial contact. We further show the effect of operating conditions, such as applied pressure, stripping current density, and temperature, on the stripping performance for different Li microstructures. Through this study, we shed light on the importance of taking microstructural features into account in predicting interfacial contact evolution during stripping of Li metal SSBs.

### 2. Modeling Approaches for Polymer-Based Electrolytes in Advanced Batteries: Bridging Processing, Structure, and Performance

> **高级电池中聚合物基电解质的建模方法：桥接处理、结构和性能**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Ying Zhang, Pei Sun, Giuseppe A. Elia, Alejandro A. Franco et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-10-02 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02246](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02246) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Polymer-based electrolytes are increasingly important in advanced batteries because of their structural tunability, mechanical compliance, and compatibility with diverse electrode and cell architectures. Their behavior depends on transport, interfacial, mechanical, and structural processes that span multiple scales and are shaped by formulation and processing history. This Perspective examines how modeling can be applied to polymer-based electrolyte systems in batteries and introduces a framework based on modeling objective, modeling basis, characteristic scale or data level, and degree of process coupling. Battery-relevant ionogel studies are used to map current modeling emphases and underrepresented directions, while polymer-electrolyte-containing composite electrodes illustrate challenges associated with process-generated microstructure and multifunctional polymer phases. Broader gaps are identified in material-state definition, experiment-model information transfer, structural representation, and process coupling. Progress therefore depends not simply on adding isolated models but on developing transparent, experimentally anchored workflows that establish testable links among formulation, processing, structure, transport, and battery-relevant performance.

Abstract 
                   Luminescent 3d3 chromium(III) complexes are emerging as highly attractive, earth-abundant alternatives to precious 4d or 5d metal complexes in photophysical and photochemical applications. However, the Laporte-forbidden nature of their d–d transitions in the visible region severely hampers the light-harvesting efficiency of conventional photoactive CrIII complexes. Abstract 
                   Tuning functional oxides by controlling ionic point defects is a well-established paradigm for designing tailored functionalities. At elevated temperatures, this is usually achieved by tuning ambient oxygen partial pressure (pO2) to regulate oxygen chemical potential and defect (e.g., protons and oxygen vacancies) concentrations. At room temperature, however, atmosphere-driven tuning is considered unfeasible due to sluggish surface kinetics and negligible ionic mobility. Here, using hydrogenated NdNiO3 as a model system, we show that ambient pO2 can in fact regulate hydrogen chemical potential and protonic defects through aerobic dehydrogenation driven by coupled anodic proton extraction and cathodic oxygen reduction in aqueous solutions. ### 3. Four-Photon
Molecular Upconversion

> **四光子
分子上转换**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Seamus S. Lowe, Maxwell Sun, Jesse A. Wisch, Kostubh Gaur et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-10-03 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c17238](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c17238) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Triplet-triplet annihilation (TTA) upconversion converts low-energy photons into higher-energy emission, but conventional TTA is limited by its two-photon nature, as the emitted photon energy cannot exceed twice the excitation photon energy. Here, a fully organic solid-state thin-film architecture cascades two TTA upconversion stages through an intermediate Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) bridge to achieve molecular four-photon upconversion. The first sensitizer/annihilator bilayer upconverts near-infrared (NIR) excitation into intermediate singlets, which are transferred through the FRET bridge to the sensitizer of the second TTA stage. The second sensitizer/annihilator bilayer performs a second TTA step to generate blue emission. The device converts 904 nm excitation into 445 nm emission, corresponding to a 1.41 eV anti-Stokes shift and an emitted photon energy 2.03 times the excitation photon energy, exceeding the maximum possible energy gain with conventional two-photon TTA. Because two quadratic TTA stages are coupled in series, the ideal low-intensity response of the four-photon device is quartic when both stages operate below threshold, providing a steeper nonlinearity desirable for nonlinear sensing. Experimentally, power-dependent measurements reveal a superquadratic slope of 3.03, followed by quadratic and linear regimes as the constituent TTA stages cross their thresholds. Independent stage measurements and time-resolved photoluminescence dynamics support the four-photon mechanism.
