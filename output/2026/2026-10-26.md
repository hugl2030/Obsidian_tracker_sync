### 1. Operando X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy Reveals Oxide Solid Electrolyte Stability against Electrochemically Plated Lithium

> **Operando X射线光电子能谱显示氧化物固体电解质对电化学镀锂的稳定性**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Huanyu Zhang, Beyzanur Erdivan, Maksym V. Kovalenko, Lars P. H. Jeurgens et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-10-26 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02717](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02717) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) has revealed many possible Li-induced interfacial reactions in solid-state electrolytes (SSEs), but most in situ studies form Li|electrolyte interfaces through non-electrochemical Li deposition, including evaporation, sputtering, or electron-beam-induced deposition. These routes may introduce additional energy that activates reaction pathways not accessed during electrochemical Li plating. Here, we use lab-based operando XPS to directly evaluate oxide solid-state electrolyte stability during electrochemical Li formation. Using this approach, we find sharply different responses for two representative oxide SSEs: NASICON-type Li1+xAlxTi2–x(PO4)3 (LATP) is reduced upon Li plating, forming a Li+/Ti3+-rich mixed-conducting interphase that partially relaxes through coupled ionic–electronic redistribution, whereas garnet-type Li7La3Zr2O12 (LLZO) shows no spectroscopically resolvable Zr or La reduction. This work represents the first direct operando XPS evaluation of SSE stability against electrochemically plated Li metal.

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Studying dynamic chemical behaviors is fundamental to understanding and regulating the dynamic responses and functions of polymer materials. However, real-time, intuitive, and selective visualization of these molecular processes─particularly when multiple dynamic motifs coexist─remains a major challenge. Herein, we introduce surface wrinkles as a visualization platform that directly translates dynamic chemical behaviors in polymers into observable macroscopic morphological changes. Using a dual-cross-linked polymer network (DCN) containing quadruple hydrogen bonds and mechanical bonds as a model system, we fabricate stable wrinkles via modulus mismatch in a bilayer architecture. ### 3. Mechanistic
Insights of Electroreductive Cobalt-Catalyzed
Asymmetric Radical Addition

电还原钴催化的见解
不对称自由基加成**

| **日期** | 2026-10-26 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c16928](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c16928) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Transition-metal-catalyzed asymmetric electroreduction has emerged as a compelling platform for enantioselective C–C bond formation, with notable advances in electroreductive cross-coupling. Translating this success to stereocontrolled radical additions across unsaturated bonds remains a formidable challenge, particularly for the construction of fully substituted stereogenic carbon centers. Herein, we report an electroreductive, cobalt-catalyzed asymmetric radical addition of diverse organohalides to α-imino esters. This protocol delivers enantioenriched α-quaternary amino acid derivatives in up to 95% ee, completely circumventing the need for stoichiometric metal reductants. Beyond its synthetic utility, the electrochemical platform uniquely enables the direct interrogation of the operative electron-transfer events. A comprehensive mechanistic study─encompassing cyclic voltammetry, in situ electrochemical EPR spectroscopy, DFT calculations, stoichiometric experiments, and radical-trapping experiments─elucidates a highly orchestrated catalytic manifold. Crucially, we demonstrate that the α-imino ester serves a dual role, acting as both a radical acceptor and a redox-active ligand for the chiral cobalt catalyst. Subsequent cathodic reduction of the resulting cobalt–imine complex generates a key low-valent species that engages the alkyl halide via a turnover-limiting single-electron transfer (SET) to produce the requisite alkyl radical. Overall, this work establishes a robust electrochemical framework that synergizes reaction innovation with fundamental mechanistic elucidation in asymmetric radical catalysis.

Abstract 
                   Selector-only memory (SOM), which integrates threshold switching and data storage within a chalcogenide layer, is a promising high-density memory technology, yet the regulation of memory characteristics and underlying mechanisms remain unclear.
