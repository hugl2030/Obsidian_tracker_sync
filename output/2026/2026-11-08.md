| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Weihao Li, Jinyu Chen, Gavin Mountjoy, Maider Zarrabeitia et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-11-07 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02828](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02828) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Layered CrSe2 reversibly accommodates approximately one potassium (K) per formula unit, but the structural origin of its rate-dependent capacity remains unresolved. Operando synchrotron X-ray diffraction (SXRD) reveals the sequential P-3m1-R3m-C2/m phase pathway during cycling at different current rates. The P-3m1/R3m transformation exhibits the lattice discontinuity and becomes incomplete when increasing the current rate, as directly observed by operando SXRD. Chemical K deintercalation and state-resolved impedance measurements provide complementary evidence for sluggish kinetics near the fully deintercalated regime. Restricting the upper cutoff voltage will sacrifice some capacity but will improve electrochemical cycling capacity retention. These findings identify a particularly rate-sensitive structural region and show how phase-resolved characterization can guide voltage-window selection for layered potassium-storage electrodes.

| **日期** | 2026-11-08 |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Molecular dynamics simulations provide insight into inhibitor-protein interactions and support rational drug design; however, their accuracy depends on reliable molecular mechanics force field parameters and coarse-grained representations that retain relevant molecular features. Although force field parameter sets for biomacromolecules are well established, the chemical diversity of small-molecule inhibitors often requires the development of system-specific parameters. Here, we present a parametrization framework integrating quantum mechanical optimization, experimental benchmarking, and small-molecule coarse-graining, demonstrated using HIV-1 inhibitors targeting multiple stages of the viral replication cycle. We provide a curated data set of CHARMM-compatible force field parameters for 12 HIV-1 inhibitors and the HIV assembly cofactor inositol hexakisphosphate (IP6), together with geometry-optimized atomic structures and ligand restraint files. The resulting parameters are validated against experimental structural and thermodynamic benchmarks, including lenacapavir binding free energy estimates, through inhibitor-bound complex simulations. We demonstrate applications of the parameter data set by quantifying inhibitor-protein interactions, including the free-energy profile of an integrase strand transfer inhibitor interacting with the viral capsid lattice, and the binding energetics of an allosteric integrase inhibitor. Furthermore, we extend the all-atom inhibitor parameter data set through a novel coarse-graining strategy for small molecules that preserves key electrostatic features. The HIV-1 inhibitor force field parameter data set is a publicly available resource that establishes a unified framework for multiscale modeling of HIV-1 inhibitors and facilitates computational and structural biology studies of drug resistance and inhibitor design. More generally, the framework presented here is broadly applicable to small-molecule parametrization and multiscale simulations in other therapeutic and biomolecular contexts.

Doping of CsPbBr

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Yan B. Vogel, Yesim Sahin, Oleksandra Ortikova, Simon C. Boehme et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-11-08 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c19292](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c19292) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Electronic doping underpins many semiconductor applications but has remained elusive for lead halide perovskite nanocrystals because their high density and low formation energy of defects easily compensate introduced dopants. Here, we use electrochemical doping as a strategy to avoid doping compensation. Electrochemical injection of electrons or holes into the nanocrystal is compensated by surface-adsorbed electrolyte counterions, therefore not compromising crystal structure. We accomplished ambipolar (n- and p-type) doped CsPbBr3 nanocrystals with charge carrier concentrations ranging from intrinsic up to 4.8 × 1017 cm–3 (corresponding to 0.25 charges per nanocrystal). We show that electrochemical doping is reversible and stable at these free carrier concentrations. The extent of doping and stability are enabled by the use of lecithin, a zwitterionic ligand that strongly binds to the surface of the CsPbBr3 nanocrystals while still allowing ion and electron transfer. Lecithin stabilizes the surface atoms by shifting the potentials for Pb2+ reduction and Br– oxidation outside of the bandgap. The inferred surface-chemistry design rules for stable electrochemical doping can also serve as a guideline for developing CsPbBr3 nanocrystals stable under high free-carrier concentrations in optoelectronic devices, such as light-emitting diodes or electronically triggered quantum light sources.

Abstract 
                   Quasi-bound states in the continuum (quasi-BICs) offer a powerful platform for refractometric sensing by combining narrow resonances with strong electromagnetic confinement, promising high spectral resolution and large figures of merit (FOM). In practice, however, these advantages are often compromised by stringent angular alignment in passive transmission/reflection interrogation. <summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Oxidation governs the stability and functional conversion of two-dimensional semiconductors, yet its propagation through layered crystals remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate the thickness-dependent oxidation of GaSe using optical spectroscopy, cross-sectional microscopy, and first-principles calculations. Experiments reveal concurrent top-down and edge-initiated oxidation, accompanied by Se redistribution and transient Ga2Se3 formation. Calculations identify Se vacancies as favorable sites for O2 adsorption and activation, which weaken neighboring Ga–Se bonds and promote oxygen incorporation. Molecular O2 faces a 4.03 eV barrier when penetrating a monolayer Ga2O3 model, compared with 0.54 eV along a GaSe van der Waals gap. This contrast supports interlayer diffusion as a plausible pathway for lateral oxidation, consistent with the pronounced thickness dependence and spatial anisotropy observed experimentally. Oxidation also yields a progression from p-type GaSe through ambipolar Ga2O3/GaSe heterostructures to n-type Ga2O3, whose devices retain stable characteristics after 300 °C air annealing.

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Controlling doping density in correlated quantum materials offers a route to tuning their electronic states, but chemical doping can introduce disorder and limits dynamic control. Here, we demonstrate optically tunable electron doping of the layered Mott insulator α-RuCl3 through photoinduced interlayer charge transfer in MoS2/α-RuCl3 heterostructures. Photoluminescence and transient absorption measurements support electron transfer to α-RuCl3, leaving holes in MoS2. The layer-separated carrier population persists for more than 100 ps with an estimated doping density tunable through the excitation fluence. Selective excitation of α-RuCl3 further reveals hole transfer to MoS2. Transient absorption microscopy yields an early time effective hole diffusion coefficient of approximately 100 cm2 s–1 in the heterostructure, supporting a predominantly unbound carrier population. These results establish photoinduced charge transfer as a means of dynamically controlling carrier doping in α-RuCl3, providing a route toward optical manipulation of correlated electronic states.
