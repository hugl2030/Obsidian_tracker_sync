### 1. Dual-Solvent Screening for Nondestructive In-Situ Optical Patterning of CsPbX3 QDs/PMMA

> **CsPbX3 QDS/PMMA无损原位光学图案的双溶剂筛选**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Hong Zhu, Yuhui Dong, Yuyao Feng, Yucong Ji et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-11-09 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02657](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02657) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Perovskite quantum dot (PQD)-polymer composites are widely pursued for display devices due to their excellent film-forming capacity, low cost, and favorable process compatibility. However, interfacial incompatibility between PQDs and polymers from mismatched surface chemistry triggers PQD aggregation and severe photoluminescence quenching, hindering microdisplay applications. Herein, a dual-solvent screening strategy is demonstrated for I-184-initiated crosslinked PQD-PMMA photoresists to balance solubility and lead-ion coordination strength. Undesired lead-solvent intermediate formation and residual solvent accumulation are suppressed to homogenize PQD dispersion and preserve optical performance. The 20 μm green patterned film retains 93% of the initial photoluminescence quantum yield with no photoluminescence peak shift and a narrow full width at half-maximum of 16.6 nm. The PQD pattern presents sharp pixel edges, 7.10% critical dimension deviation, 97.1% replication rate, and intersection over union of 0.92. This solvent engineering enables the revitalization of conventional polymers and addresses bottlenecks in photolithographic patterning of PQD-polymer composites.

### 1. Catalytic Interphases
Enable Sulfur Redox Catalysis
in All-Solid-State Lithium–Sulfur Batteries

> **催化间相

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Mingyang Jiang, Jiwei Shi, Zhonghao Hu, Tianyu Yin et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-11-09 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c13825](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c13825) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Weak solid–solid contact with limited chemical and transport continuity is a common bottleneck in solid-state batteries, particularly in all-solid-state lithium–sulfur batteries (ASSLSBs), where Li2S/S8 conversion requires catalytic acceleration but lacks a continuous solid-state catalytic pathway. Here, we propose an interphase catalysis strategy for solid–solid sulfur conversion by transforming discrete catalyst/reactant interfaces into a chemically integrated reaction interphase. WO3 is identified as a suitable oxide to construct such a metastable mixed-anion S–W–O catalytic interphase on Li2S based on the work-function difference between the metal oxide catalyst and Li2S/S8, which serves as a practical screening rationale for evaluating the relative tendency toward sulfur–oxygen coordination exchange. The interphase contains unsaturated W centers and a polarized electronic environment, enabling coupled electron/Li+ transport and dynamically regulating sulfur redox through coupled sulfur-species exchange. This integrated interphase further stabilizes cathode interfaces during repeated Li2S/S8 conversion. As a result, the optimized cathode sustains stable cycling over 15000 cycles at 10 C and a temperature of 60 °C. A record-high Li2S loading of 30 mg cm–2 with an areal capacity of 21.2 mAh cm–2 is also achieved, demonstrating interphase catalysis as an effective route to overcome solid-state sulfur-conversion limitations in practical ASSLSBs.

on Nonenzymatic RNA Copying

关于非酶RNA复制**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Jahmyl Essex, Xiwen Jia, Jian Zhang, Jack W. Szostak |
| **日期** | 2026-11-09 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c14723](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c14723) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   It has long been known that L-nucleotides inhibit the nonenzymatic copying of D-RNA templates, suggesting that nucleotide homochirality must have been attained before the emergence of RNA replication and the Origin of Life. Recent advances in nonenzymatic RNA copying, including the use of 2-aminoimidazole–activated monomers, and the elucidation of a reaction mechanism involving 2-aminoimidazolium-bridged dinucleotides, have inspired us to revisit the effects of L-nucleotides on the copying of D-RNA. We find that 2-AI-activated D- and L-nucleotides form heterochiral bridged dinucleotides at a slightly faster rate than homochiral bridged dinucleotides. 2-AI-bridged homochiral L/L dinucleotides bind D-RNA primer-template complexes with a higher Km than D/D bridged dinucleotides and lead to much slower D-RNA primer extension. Heterochiral bridged dinucleotides can bind surprisingly well to D-RNA primer-template complexes but react slowly, leading to competitive inhibition. Even more strikingly, an L-nucleotide at the 3′ end of a D-RNA primer is a chain terminator. X-ray crystallography shows that the 3′-hydroxyl of the terminal L-nucleotide faces away from the phosphate of the incoming reactive substrate, explaining the failure of continued primer extension. We also show that L-nucleotides can be incorporated in a D-RNA stem-loop via loop-closing ligation, removing them from the replicating pool. Together, our results provide a kinetic and structural framework for understanding why a high level, if not complete, homochirality is required for nonenzymatic RNA replication.

### 3. Dissecting the Influence
of Molecular Charge on Hydrophobicity

> **剖析影响力
疏水性的分子电荷**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | JACS |
| **作者** | Ben Iddon, Christopher A. Hunter |
| **日期** | 2026-11-09 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/jacs.6c19996](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c19996) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Interactions of hydrophobic environments and ionic solutes are important in a range of biological processes, but the effect of molecular charge on hydrophobicity remains opaque. The interaction of isostructural solutes with the hydrophobic cavity of a cyclodextrin (γ-CD) in water was used to directly compare the hydrophobicities of anionic, neutral, and cationic species. Complex formation between γ-CD and PPh4+, BPh4– and a neutral analogue (CPh4 derivative) was investigated by ITC and NMR spectroscopy at different temperatures. The anion and neutral analogue bind γ-CD with similar association constants, but binding of the cation is 104-fold weaker. The thermodynamic signatures ΔH° and ΔCp° are similar for the anion and neutral analogue, but quite different for the cation. There is almost perfect entropy-enthalpy compensation in all three cases, but binding of the anion and neutral analogue is enthalpy-driven, while binding of the cation is entropy-driven. Substituents increase the affinity of tetraarylborates (up to 108 M–1, the highest reported value for native cyclodextrins), and substituent effects correlate well with the Hansch hydrophobicity parameter. In reversed-phase HPLC experiments that provide an alternative measure of hydrophobicity, the behavior of BPh4– is again similar to CPh4 and quite different from PPh4+, which is less retained by hydrophobic stationary phases. Asymmetry in the behavior of ions has implications for understanding partition coefficients of charged compounds, dipole potentials in lipid membranes, and the chaotropic effect. The experiments indicate that aqueous solvation of organic cations is much stronger than that of organic anions (by &gt;20 kJ mol–1 for tetraphenyl compounds).

| **日期** | 2026-11-06 |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

The chemical recycling of polyolefins (PO) via pyrolysis offers a circular pathway for plastic waste management, yet industrial implementation remains hindered by high energy demands and poor product selectivity. Here, we report an Fe-catalyzed pyrolysis process that achieves 97% PO conversion and a 94% yield of C6 to C35 liquid hydrocarbons. By using a zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF)–derived, N-doped porous carbon-supported iron single-atom catalyst (Fe
                     1 
                    @NC) in combination with a strategic temperature gradient control, we overcome traditional thermolysis limitations. The process was further demonstrated on a kilogram scale, supporting the practical scalability of this catalytic system. Mechanistic investigations reveal that the rate-determining step is an acid base–promoted dehydrogenation of the polymer backbone. Specifically, electronic synergy between the single iron centers and the carbon support stabilizes reactive carbocation intermediates, facilitating their rapid decomposition into high-value olefins and alkanes. These findings provide a scalable and highly efficient catalytic framework for the upcycling of plastic waste into liquid hydrocarbon fractions and chemical feedstocks.
