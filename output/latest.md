### 1. Liquid Metal Interlayers Enable Zero-Pressure Sulfide Solid Electrolyte Lithium Batteries

> **液态金属夹层实现了零压硫化物固态电解质锂电池**

| **日期** | 2026-11-26 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02793](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02793) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Lithium metal anodes are essential for high-energy-density batteries with solid-state electrolytes (SSEs), but the lithium-SSE interface suffers from contact loss and current constriction during cycling. External stack pressure can enhance contact retention but adds system-level limitations and accelerates lithium filament penetration. Here, a room-temperature liquid sodium–potassium (NaK) interlayer is demonstrated to stabilize the lithium–sulfide SSE interface under nominally zero stack pressure by combining stress-free morphological adaptability with Li transport capability. In situ X-ray computed tomography and other analytical methods reveal that the liquid layer reconfigures to preserve interfacial contact during Li plating/stripping. Finite Li solubility in the NaK also creates a dissolved-Li-mediated electrochemical pathway that supports Li stripping from a distant reservoir. The dual mechanical and transport functions of NaK enable stable full-cell cycling (600 cycles, &gt;2 mAh cm–2) with layered-oxide composite cathodes under nominally zero applied stack pressure. These findings highlight a fundamental interfacial design concept based on a compliant interlayer that accommodates morphological evolution while supporting Li transport under minimal mechanical confinement.

### 2. Programming Sodium-Storage Kinetics through Linear Quinone Oligomerization

> **通过线性醌寡聚化调控钠储存动力学**

| | |
|---|---|
| **期刊** | ACS Energy Letters |
| **作者** | Wenwen Deng, Tingting Jiang, Longfei Li, Jiyu Huang et al. |
| **日期** | 2026-11-26 |
| **DOI** | [10.1021/acsenergylett.6c03180](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.6c03180) |

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

**关键词**：—

<details>
<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Quinone-based organic cathodes enable reversible multielectron redox reactions but suffer from dissolution and sluggish sodium-storage kinetics. Here, two structurally homologous linear quinone oligomers, biphenyl–quinone dimer (2BQ) and terphenyl–quinone trimer (3BQ), are compared to elucidate how backbone extension regulates sodium storage. Compared with 2BQ, 3BQ exhibits reduced crystallinity, enhanced electrochemical utilization, a narrower HOMO–LUMO gap, and lower oxidation reorganization energy. Consequently, 3BQ delivers a reversible capacity of 350 mA h g–1 at 0.05C and retains 80% of its capacity after 500 cycles at 1C, with enhanced rate capability and cycling stability. Operando FTIR and state-dependent XPS reveal reversible C=O/C–O–Na conversion, while density functional theory calculations elucidate an extended multielectron sodiation sequence. These findings establish controlled linear quinone oligomerization as an effective, rational molecular design strategy to reduce apparent dissolution tendency and improve carbonyl-site utilization and sodium-storage kinetics, providing insights into the development of advanced organic cathodes for rechargeable sodium-ion batteries.

Abstract 
                   Raising the operating current density can effectively reduce the high cost of proton exchange membrane water electrolyzers (PEMWEs), but this approach is limited by rapid electrode degradation. Abstract 
                   Thermally generated magnons enable heat-driven spin transport in magnetic insulators, yet the relative importance of microscopic mechanisms governing their propagation remains incompletely understood. Here, we investigate thermal magnon transport in low-damping Li0.5Al1.0Fe1.5O4/Pt nanodevices by using a nonlocal spin Seebeck geometry that separates magnon transport from local thermoelectric effects.
