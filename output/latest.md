Abstract 
                   Nanoarchitecturing improves capacity utilization and mitigates mechanical stress of Sn anodes for elevated energy density sodium-ion batteries (SIBs), yet substantial surface energy induces nanoparticle aggregation. Abstract 
                   Proton-exchange membrane (PEM) water electrolyzers must operate for 80,000 h, and membrane degradation limits this lifetime. Because electroosmotic drag carries degradation products toward the cathode, the fluoride emission rate, the standard diagnostic, underrepresents anode-side and bulk degradation. It also misses larger fluorocarbon fragments (LC-MS); after correction for compression, about 80% of the excess measured thinning (SEM) over fluoride-derived thinning at 1 A cm−2 is chemical loss. Depth profiles at both membrane faces in time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry, with transport analysis, indicate anisotropic degradation across the full PEM thickness, consistent with cathode-generated hydrogen peroxide diffusing toward the anode, most strongly at 0.5–1.0 A cm−2. The estimated peroxide production rate is six times the molar rate of membrane loss. To reach 80,000 h, our results point to two design targets: oxygen crossover an order of magnitude below today's 1% benchmark, and peroxide-decomposition catalysts distributed throughout the membrane bulk.

**🔑 核心价值**：（暂无）

<summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   Localized high-concentration electrolytes (LHCEs) have been extensively investigated to improve rechargeable battery performance, yet most rely on costly fluorinated diluents. Herein, we develop a 1M LHCE using toluene as nonfluorinated and low-cost diluent for wide-temperature sodium-ion batteries (SIBs). The 1M LHCE features a compressed solvation structure and preserved ion-transport pathways, enabling the antimonene alloy anode to deliver stable capacities of 555.2 mAh g–1 over 600 cycles at 25 °C and 573.2 and 512.3 mAh g–1 at 50 and –20 °C, respectively. <summary>📄 英文摘要</summary>

Abstract 
                   All-solid-state batteries (ASSBs) hold great promise for next-generation energy storage owing to their high safety and energy density, yet their performance critically depends on solid electrolytes (SEs) with high ionic conductivity and structural robustness. Mixed-anion chemistry, exemplified by the Cl/O system, has emerged as an effective approach for designing halide-based superionic conductors, where partial oxygen incorporation distorts the regular halide framework and enhances Li+ mobility.
